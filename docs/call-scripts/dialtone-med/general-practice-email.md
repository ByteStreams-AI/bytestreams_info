# DialTone.Med Email Sequence: General Practice

For primary care, family medicine, internal medicine, and nurse-practitioner practices
(CRM business type **General Practice**). Send to the contact on the lead: the authorized official,
or the practice manager if you've learned who that is.

Placeholders: `[first_name]`, `[practice_name]`, `[city]`, `[Your Name]`, `[Day/Time A]`, `[Day/Time B]`.

## Email 1: First Touch (Day 1)

**Subject options** (pick one; keep it lower case and plain):

- `phones at [practice_name]`
- `the 8:30 phone rush`
- `quick question, [first_name]`

> Hi [first_name],
>
> Most practices tell us the phones are hardest at the open and right after lunch. Calls roll to voicemail,
> and some of those patients book somewhere else.
>
> DialTone.Med answers every call to [practice_name], checks who's calling, and texts the patient a secure link
> to finish the request: move an appointment, pay a balance, update their insurance. Refill requests go to
> your team in the patient's own words, and balances and medications are never said aloud.
>
> Would a 15-minute demo be worth it? You'd hear it handle a patient call.
>
> [Your Name]
> DialTone.Med

## Email 2: Prior-Auth Angle (Day 4)

**Subject:** `re: phones at [practice_name]` (send as a reply in the same thread)

> Hi [first_name],
>
> One more thought. For a lot of practices the heaviest calls aren't scheduling. They're "has my prior
> authorization come through yet?"
>
> Our agent answers those calls with the status only ("one approved, one in review"), never the drug or
> procedure, and texts the details. The status comes from AuthPulse, our prior-auth tracker, which is in early
> access. It puts every request on a deadline clock and flags late decisions and approvals about to expire.
>
> Worth 15 minutes to see both?
>
> [Your Name]

## Email 3: Close the Loop (Day 9)

>
> [Your Name]

## After a Call: "Send Me Information"

**Subject:** `DialTone.Med for [practice_name], as promised`

> Hi [first_name],
>
> Thanks for taking my call. As promised, here's the short version.
>
> DialTone.Med answers every call, verifies the patient, and texts a secure link that finishes the request:
>
> - **Appointments:** book, reschedule, or cancel, with confirmation by text and email.
> - **Billing:** the patient sees the balance and pays by card from the link. Nothing is said aloud.
> - **Refills:** taken in the patient's own words and queued for your team. The decision stays with the prescriber.
> - **Prior-auth status:** status only on the call; details by secure link.
> - **Insurance:** the patient enters new plan details, and your staff review the eligibility result.
>
> You keep your phone numbers, and no EHR integration is needed to start.
>
> The easiest way to judge it is to hear a call. Does [Day/Time A] or [Day/Time B] work?
>
> [Your Name]

## Notes

- Send Email 2 and 3 only if there's no reply. Stop the sequence as soon as anyone answers, including "no."
- Mark **Emailed** in the CRM after Email 1, and log replies in the notes.
