# DialTone.Med Email Sequence: Specialty Pharmacy

For specialty and infusion pharmacies (CRM business type **Specialty Pharmacy**). Send to the contact on the
lead: the authorized official, pharmacist in charge, or the operations or call-center manager.

Do not open with missed calls. Specialty pharmacies already answer nearly every call. The angle is **staff time**.

Placeholders: `[first_name]`, `[pharmacy_name]`, `[Your Name]`, `[Day/Time A]`, `[Day/Time B]`.

## Email 1: First Touch (Day 1)

**Subject options:**

- `"where's my refill?" calls`
- `staff time on the phones at [pharmacy_name]`
- `quick question, [first_name]`

> Hi [first_name],
>
> Specialty pharmacies answer almost every call; URAC's latest report puts abandonment around 3%. The cost
> is who's answering: pharmacists and technicians spending hours on refills, authorization status, and
> "what do I owe."
>
> DialTone.Med answers those calls for [pharmacy_name], verifies the patient, and texts a secure link with the
> detail. Refill requests go to your team in the patient's own words, and drug names and balances are never
> said aloud.
>
> Would a 15-minute demo be worth it?
>
> [Your Name]
> DialTone.Med

## Email 2: Prior-Auth Angle (Day 4)

**Subject:** `re: [the subject of Email 1]`

> Hi [first_name],
>
> One more thought. How many prior authorizations does [pharmacy_name] have open right now, and how many
> are past their due date?
>
> AuthPulse, our prior-auth tracker (in early access), keeps every request from every prescriber and plan in
> one list on a deadline clock. It flags late decisions, denials sent without a specific reason, and
> approvals about to expire. With our voice agent, patients who call to ask get the status, never the drug
> name, and a text when it changes.
>
> Worth 15 minutes?
>
> [Your Name]

## Email 3: Close the Loop (Day 9)

**Subject:** `re: [the subject of Email 1]`

>

## After a Call: "Send Me Information"

**Subject:** `DialTone.Med for [pharmacy_name], as promised`

> Hi [first_name],
>
> Thanks for taking my call. Here's the short version.
>
> DialTone.Med answers routine patient calls, verifies the caller, and texts a secure link with the detail:
>
> - **Refill requests:** taken in the patient's own words and queued for your team. The agent never approves a refill.
> - **Prior-auth status:** status only on the call, never the drug name; details by secure link, and a text when it changes.
> - **Billing:** the patient sees the balance and pays by card from the link. No card numbers read to staff.
> - **Insurance:** the patient enters new plan details, and your staff review the eligibility result.
> - **Caregivers:** family members can call with only the permissions your staff grant.
>
> **AuthPulse** (early access) tracks every prior authorization your team submits, with deadline clocks and
> flags for late, expiring, and unexplained denials. It doesn't submit or decide anything.
>
> Does [Day/Time A] or [Day/Time B] work for 15 minutes?
>
> [Your Name]

## Notes

- Do not claim DialTone.Med helps meet URAC or ACHC call targets. That has not been confirmed.
- Send Email 2 and 3 only if there's no reply. Stop as soon as anyone answers, including "no."
- Mark **Emailed** in the CRM after Email 1, and log replies in the notes.
