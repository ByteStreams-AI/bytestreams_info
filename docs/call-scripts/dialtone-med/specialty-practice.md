# DialTone.Med Cold Call Script: Specialty Practice

For specialist practices such as oral surgery, dermatology, orthopedics, cardiology, and GI
(CRM business type **Specialty Practice**).

## Objective

Do not explain the whole product. The call has three jobs:

1. Reach the owner, physician lead, or practice administrator.
2. Name the problem specialists live with: referral and consult calls that can't wait, plus the
   prior-authorization status calls that follow almost every procedure.
3. Book a 15-minute demo where they hear the agent handle a patient call.

Keep it under five minutes.

## Before the Call

Check the lead in the CRM:

- **Contact name and title.** NPI imports carry the authorized official in the contact name and the title in the notes.
- **Contact phone.** The official's direct line, if imported. Try it first.
- **Specialty.** In the clinical specialty field (for example "Dentist, Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery").
  Use the specialty's language: consults, procedures, surgery dates, pre-op.
- **Website.** Look for a consult-request form, online scheduling, or a referral page.

## Getting Past the Front Desk

> Hi, this is [Your Name] with DialTone.Med. I'm trying to reach [contact_name]. Is he available?

If asked what it's about:

> We help specialty practices answer every patient call, including consult requests and prior-auth status
> questions. I'd like to set up a short demo with [contact_name]. What's the best way to get 15 minutes with him?

If the decision maker isn't available, ask who manages the front office and the best time to call back.

## First 30 Seconds

> Hi [contact_name], this is [Your Name] with DialTone.Med. How are you?
>
> I'll be quick. For specialty practices, a missed call is often a new referral, a patient who was sent to
> you and picked the next office on the list. And after a procedure is scheduled, the phones fill up with
> "has my authorization come through yet?"
>
> DialTone.Med answers every call, verifies the patient, and texts a secure link to finish the request:
> scheduling, prior-auth status, billing, insurance. Your staff stay on the patients in front of them.
>
> I'd like to set up 15 minutes next week so you can hear it on a call. Would that be worth a look?

Stop and let them respond.

## Discovery Questions

Ask one or two.

1. When a referred patient calls and can't get through, what happens?
2. How many prior authorizations does the practice handle in a week?
3. How much staff time goes to patients calling to ask about authorization status?
4. Who tracks prior-auth deadlines today, and with what: the EHR, a spreadsheet, a whiteboard?
5. How do patients pay their portion before or after a procedure?
6. Do your calls spike around surgery schedules or particular days?

## The Five Workflows

Use only the ones the prospect asks about, or the one that matches their biggest pain. Every workflow
follows the same pattern: **the call identifies the patient, a secure texted link carries the detail, and
staff see every request in one console.** Anything the agent can't finish waits as a message in the
caller's own words until staff close it.

### Prior Authorization Status

- **Patient says:** "Has my prior authorization been approved yet?"
- **Agent:** after verifying the caller, says only the status ("one approved, one still in review"), never the
  procedure. Details, dates, and the next step go by secure link.
- **Patient:** gets a text when the status changes: approved, denied, more information needed, or an appeal decided.
- **Left for staff:** nothing. No status call to return. The status comes from AuthPulse (below).

### Appointments

- **Patient says:** "I need to move my appointment on Thursday."
- **Agent:** checks who's calling, then texts a secure link with open times.
- **Patient:** books, reschedules, or cancels from the link and gets a confirmation by text and email.
- **Left for staff:** nothing. The change is already in the schedule.

### Billing and Payment

- **Patient says:** "How much do I owe, and can I pay it now?"
- **Agent:** never says the amount aloud. It texts a secure link with the balance.
- **Patient:** pays by card from the link and gets a receipt by text and email. Nobody reads a card number to staff.
- **Left for staff:** nothing.

### Insurance Updates

- **Patient says:** "I have new insurance. Can you update it?"
- **Agent:** texts a secure link.
- **Patient:** types in the carrier, member ID, and group number.
- **Left for staff:** an eligibility result to review.

### Refill Requests

- **Patient says:** "I need a refill on my blood pressure medication."
- **Agent:** takes the request in the patient's own words and tells them it has gone to the care team.
  It never approves a refill or promises one.
- **Left for staff:** one request to review in the console. The decision stays with the prescriber.

### Also Worth Knowing

- **Caregiver access.** Family members can call with only the permissions staff grant, such as appointments
  and prior-auth status but not payments.
- **Getting started.** The practice keeps its phone numbers. No EHR integration is needed to start; staff work
  from the console, and the EHR can be connected later.

## AuthPulse: Prior Authorization Tracking

AuthPulse is our prior-auth tracker for staff. It works on its own or with the voice agent, which reads
patient status from it. **It is in early access**, so say so: "it's in early access, and early customers
help shape it."

For specialty practices this is often the stronger opener: nearly every procedure needs an authorization, and every authorization brings status calls.

What it does:

- **One list, every request.** Every prior authorization the team is waiting on, sorted by what needs attention first.
- **Decision clocks.** Shows when each plan's answer is due where a federal timeframe applies (for example, 72
  hours for standard Medicare Part D drug requests, 7 days for Medicare Advantage medical services),
  and turns amber, then red, as the deadline passes.
- **Flags.** Late or due-today decisions; denials that came without a specific reason, which certain plans must
  give, so staff can ask before deciding on an appeal; approvals expiring within 30 days, so renewals start on time.
- **A record for the plan.** Each request keeps its timeline: submitted, delivery confirmed, calls to the plan,
  decision, appeal. A turnaround report shows each plan's record over 90 days.
- **Getting requests in.** Staff add them by hand or upload a spreadsheet; a scheduled EHR export can feed it
  automatically. Teams that fax can add a packet-and-fax assistant that builds the packet and sends the fax.

What it does not do, and you must not imply it does:

- It does not submit, decide, or appeal prior authorizations. The team submits the way it does today.
- A status in AuthPulse is a fact relayed from the plan, never a prediction.

Ask how they track deadlines today. If the answer is a spreadsheet, the EHR inbox, or "we just know," AuthPulse is the meeting.

Useful lines:

> Prior auth isn't really the form. It's the chasing: status calls, deadlines nobody's watching, denials no one
> has time to appeal. AuthPulse puts every request on a clock and tells your staff which ones need them today.

> Most denials that get appealed are overturned, at least in Medicare Advantage. Very few get appealed, because
> nobody has time to spot them. AuthPulse flags the ones worth a second look.

## Close for the Meeting

### Standard Close

> The best way to judge it is to hear a call. It takes 15 minutes, and we'll run your own numbers on call
> volume and staff time. Would [Day/Time A] or [Day/Time B] work better?

### Prior-Auth Close

> Since authorizations are the bigger issue, let us walk you through AuthPulse: every request, every deadline,
> and patients getting their status without a callback. Is [Day/Time A] or [Day/Time B] better?

Always offer two times. Confirm the email address and send the invitation before you hang up.

## Objection Responses

### "Our EHR already tracks prior auths."

> Many do. The gap we usually find is the phone side: patients calling to ask, and staff stopping to look it
> up. The agent answers those status calls for you. We can also show whether AuthPulse would catch deadlines
> the EHR doesn't flag.

### "We have an answering service."

> That covers after hours. During the day, the issue is the referral that rings out while everyone's busy.
> DialTone.Med answers it and gets the patient scheduled, rather than taking a message.

### "Patients won't want to talk to AI."

> The agent's job is to finish the request quickly, not to keep them talking. Anything clinical or anything it
> can't handle goes to your staff in the patient's own words. Hearing a call is the best way to judge.

### "What about HIPAA?"

> We work as your business associate under a signed agreement. The agent never says a procedure, medication, or
> balance aloud; those go to the patient's secure link. Calls are recorded only with consent and deleted after 30 days.

### "How much does it cost?"

> It depends on your call volume and which workflows you'd use, so I'd rather not guess. On the demo we'll
> look at your numbers and give you an exact figure.

### "Send me information."

> Happy to. Is the bigger issue the phones themselves, or prior authorizations?

After the answer:

> I'll send that today. Let's also hold 15 minutes so you can see it. Is [Day/Time A] or [Day/Time B] better?

### "I'm not the right person."

> Thanks. Who runs the front office or handles authorizations? I'll reach out to them directly.

## After the Call

- Set the CRM status: **Contacted**, **Follow-up Required**, or **Demo Scheduled**.
- Mark **Phoned**.
- Note the specialty, prior-auth volume, how they track authorizations, and who decides.
